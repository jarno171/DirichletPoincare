The analytic Poincaré inequality
on planar regions — formalized

A guided tour of the Lean proofs in DirichletPoincare/. For which bounded regions G ⊂ ℂ is every holomorphic function controlled by its derivative, ∫G|f|² ≤ K ∫G|f′|²? Starlike regions: yes. A chain of discs joined by hair-thin necks: no. Every statement below is fully machine-checked.

Based on Jarno Varis, Dirichlet space and analytic Poincaré inequality (2013, in Finnish). The simulations are numerical illustrations of what the Lean files prove; the source of every proof is browsable at the bottom of the page.

1The objects

Everything lives in the plane ℂ ≅ ℝ² with Lebesgue area measure dA. All integrals are lower Lebesgue integrals in ℝ≥0∞, so “finite” just means < ⊤.

Dirichlet space D(G)

f holomorphic on G, f(z₀)=0, and finite energy

‖f‖²D = ∫G |f′(z)|² dA < ∞

dirichletEnergy, IsInDirichletSpace

Bergman norm

The plain area-integral of |f|²

‖f‖²B = ∫G |f(z)|² dA

bergmanNormSq; the thesis' D(G) ⊂ B(G) is DirichletSubsetBergman

Poincaré (2,2) inequality

One constant works for the whole space

∃ K < ∞ ∀ f∈D(G): ‖f‖²B ≤ K ‖f‖²D

PoincareInequality G z₀

def PoincareInequality (G : Set ℂ) (z₀ : ℂ) : Prop :=
  ∃ K : ℝ≥0∞, K < ⊤ ∧
    ∀ f : ℂ → ℂ, IsInDirichletSpace G z₀ f → bergmanNormSq G f ≤ K * dirichletEnergy G f

2Starlike regions satisfy it Theorem 2.13

A region is starlike if some centre w₀ sees every point: the segment from w₀ to z stays inside for all z. Convex regions are starlike, but so are stars and L-shapes. Move the blue centre and the gold point. The green shaded set is the kernel (all valid centres; it is the intersection of the half-planes left of each edge); a region is starlike exactly when this kernel is non-empty.

How the Lean proof goes Starlike{Aux,Main}.lean

The thesis proves 2.13 via the deep Lemma 2.10. The Lean proof is direct and uses polar coordinates around the centre.

1 · Ray estimate

Integrate f′ along a ray and use Cauchy–Schwarz (ray_estimate):

|f(w₀+ρu) − f(w₀)|² ≤ ρ ∫₀^ρ |f′(w₀+su)|² ds

2 · Polar coordinates

Starlikeness keeps the whole ray inside G, so a radial Hardy-type inequality (radial_bound) bounds

∫G |f−f(w₀)|² ≤ R³ ∫ (…) ∫G|f′|²-weighted

3 · The singular centre

The weight blows up as ρ→0. Near w₀ it is controlled by the area mean-value inequality (next section) applied to f′:

|f′(u)|² ≤ E / area(B(u,δ))

4 · Move the base point

The constant |f(w₀)|² is controlled using f(z₀)=0 on a small disc around z₀, giving

‖f‖²B ≤ 2‖f−f(w₀)‖² + 2|f(w₀)|²·area(G) ≤ K ‖f‖²D
theorem poincare_of_starlike {G : Set ℂ} (hbdd : Bornology.IsBounded G)
    (hS : IsStarlikeRegion G) {z₀ : ℂ} (hz₀ : z₀ ∈ G) :
    PoincareInequality G z₀

Theorem 2.22 extends this to piecewise starlike regions G = E ∪ G₁ ∪ … ∪ Gₙ (null set E): apply 2.13 on each piece, then bound the value of f at each piece's base point by a chain of discs across the connected region.

3The missing tool: an area mean-value inequality AreaMeanValue.lean

Mathlib has the mean-value property on circles, but not on discs. The Lean proof computes in polar coordinates and averages the circle identity over the radius. For holomorphic g on a disc:

|g(w)|² · area(B(w,r)) ≤ ∫B(w,r) |g|² dA   (sq_mul_volume_le)

Try it: the circle average and the area average of a holomorphic f both equal f(w) exactly (up to quadrature error); for the non-holomorphic |z|² they do not.

Proof ingredients in Lean: lintegral_ball_polar (polar form of a disc integral), circle_sq_le (Cauchy–Schwarz on each circle plus Mathlib's circle mean-value property), then integrate over ρ.

4Inclusion ⇒ bounded Lemma 2.10 Corollary 2.12

If every f∈D(G) happens to lie in B(G), then automatically ‖f‖B ≤ √C ‖f‖D for one constant C — a closed-graph-type phenomenon. The Lean proof applies Mathlib's open-mapping machinery; step through it.

theorem inclusion_bounded {G : Set ℂ} (hR : IsRegion G) (_hbdd : Bornology.IsBounded G)
    {z₀ : ℂ} (hz₀ : z₀ ∈ G) (h : DirichletSubsetBergman G z₀) :
    ∃ C : ENNReal, C < ⊤ ∧
      ∀ f : ℂ → ℂ, IsInDirichletSpace G z₀ f → bergmanNormSq G f ≤ C * dirichletEnergy G f

Corollary 2.12 is the immediate consequence: on a bounded region, D(G) ⊂ B(G) ⇔ the Poincaré (2,2) inequality. The Lean version of Lemma 2.10 needs IsRegion G: on a disconnected set a function can be constant on a component not containing z₀, where its energy is zero.

5Conformal maps and multipliers Theorem 2.5 Lemma 2.4

For a simply connected region G = φ(U) with Riemann map φ from the unit disc U, the following are equivalent: (1) D(G)⊂B(G) · (2) z·f ∈ D(G) for every f∈D(G) · (3) φ·g ∈ D(U) for every g∈D(U) · (4) φ′·g ∈ B(U) for every g∈D(U). The engine is that the Dirichlet energy is conformally invariant:

∫G |f′|² dA = ∫U |(f∘φ)′|² dA    (area element transforms by |φ′|²)

Below: the unit disc with its polar grid, shaded by the Jacobian |φ′|², and the image region. The numbers compare the two sides computed independently (the left one on a grid inside the image curve).

The new ingredient: injective ⇒ φ′ ≠ 0

To transport functions back from U to G one needs a holomorphic inverse. Mathlib has neither “injective holomorphic maps have nonvanishing derivative” nor holomorphy of the inverse, so both are proved in RiemannAux.lean: if φ′(a)=0 then locally φ−φ(a) = ((z−a)h(z))k with k ≥ 2 and an analytic k-th root h; the local open-mapping property of w=(z−a)h and a primitive root of unity give two distinct points with the same image — contradicting injectivity. (1)↔(2) and (3)↔(4) follow from one multiplier lemma isMultiplier_iff: a bounded holomorphic c multiplies D(S) into itself ⇔ c′f ∈ B(S) for all f∈D(S).

6A bounded region where it fails Theorem 3.2 Theorem 3.14

Take chambers Dₖ = B(cₖ, cₖ/4) with cₖ = 1/mₖ² along the real axis, joined by rectangular necks of half-height hₖ = 1/mₖ¹⁰. The integers mₖ grow so fast (they are chosen recursively, Params.lean) that the necks are essentially invisible. The test functions gm(z) = (1−z²)m are ≈ 0 near z=1 and ≈ 1 near the small chambers.

The region (illustrative growth mk+1 = λ·mk)

The test functions

∫G|gm′|² ≤ 10/m⁵   (energy_regionS_le),    ∫G|gm − c|² ≥ (1/64)·area(Dm) = π/(1024 m⁴)

Here m = mn+1 is chosen after the previous chambers so that the first n necks contribute little energy (mm_spec). The ratio of the two bounds is ≥ π·m/10240, which exceeds any K for large m — so no constant works. Drag m below: |gm| and the energy density |gm′|² along the real axis.

|gm(x)||gm′(x)|² (rescaled) chamber Dm at x ≈ 1/m² (gm ≈ 1)chamber at z = 1 (gm ≈ 0)

Whatever the constant c is, gm−c is ≥ 1/8 in absolute value on one of the two chambers (big_diff_or): if |c| ≥ ¼ it is large near z=1, else near the small chamber.

Theorem 3.14: the boundary is rectifiable

The frontier is {0} ∪ the circles ∂Dₖ ∪ the boundaries of the necks. Each piece is the image of an interval under a 1-Lipschitz map (lipschitzWith_circleMap, hausdorffMeasure_image_le), and the lengths are dominated by 40·4⁻ᵏ, so the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure is finite.

7(2,2) holds, but Lq and L∞ fail Theorem 3.21

Now take the starlike region G = D₀ ∪ ⋃ₖ Sₖ: a disc around 1 plus thin horizontal rectangles Sₖ = (cₖ,1)×(−hₖ,hₖ). Every segment from 1 to a point of G stays inside — hover to test — so Theorem 2.13 gives the (2,2) inequality. Yet:

  • the energy of gm is still ≤ 10/m⁵ (G lies inside the larger region regionS for which this bound is proved);
  • on a rectangle of area ≥ 1/(8m¹²) at x ≈ 1/m², |gm−c| ≥ 1/8, so ‖gm−c‖L^q ≳ m−12/q;
  • the Poincaré (q,2) inequality ‖f‖q ≤ K‖f′‖2 would need m−12/q ≲ K·m−5/2 — false for large m as soon as 12/q < 5/2.

Where does it break? A log–log picture

Lower bound for ‖gm−c‖q versus K‖gm′‖2 as functions of m. Once the lower bound (mint) rises above the upper bound (coral) the inequality is violated. In the thesis the exponent is q = 2p/(2−p); the Lean proof shows the failure for every q ≥ 8, i.e. p ≥ 8/5, with a single p₀ for all K.

For q = ∞ (the (‖·‖∞, 2) inequality) the lower bound is the constant 1/8 and the upper bound K·√10·m−5/2 → 0, so it fails as well (not_poincareLp_top_regionStar).

theorem exists_region_with_lp_poincare_failures :
    ∃ G : Set ℂ, Bornology.IsBounded G ∧ IsRegion G ∧
      (∀ z₀ ∈ G, PoincareInequality G z₀) ∧
      (∀ K : ℝ≥0, ∃ p₀ : ℝ, 1 < p₀ ∧ p₀ < 2 ∧
        ∀ p : ℝ, p₀ ≤ p → p < 2 → ∀ z₀ ∈ G,
          ¬ PoincareLpWithConst G z₀ (ENNReal.ofReal (2 * p / (2 - p))) 2 K) ∧
      (∀ z₀ ∈ G, ¬ PoincareLp G z₀ ∞ 2)

8Where the Lean version differs from the thesis

Different proofs

  • 2.13 avoids Lemma 2.10 (polar coordinates + area mean-value).
  • 2.22 via 2.13 on every piece and a chain of discs; Lemma 2.20 is proved but not needed.
  • 2.10 by the open mapping theorem on the graph in L²×L², not the closed graph theorem.
  • 3.2 uses discs-and-necks (not sectors) and a direct contradiction.
  • 3.21 uses a disc-and-rectangles region instead of the thesis' sector staircase.

Stronger / modified statements

  • 3.21: one p₀ = 8/5 works for all K.
  • 2.10 needs IsRegion G (false for disconnected G).
  • 3.15: needs q > 0; ‖f‖∞ > 0 is not needed.
  • 3.18: existential a₀ per r₀, weaker than a shared a.
  • 3.1: extra hypothesis 1 ≤ n; stand-alone, not used by 3.2.

Not formalized

  • Corollary 2.24 (a constant on discs) was dropped on purpose.
  • Most of the spiral domain (thesis §4): only the strip and the injectivity of exp(i·) on it.
  • Background remarks and examples.
